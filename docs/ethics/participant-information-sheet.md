# Participant Information Sheet

## Study Title

VERISSIMO: Evaluating a decision-support web prototype for explaining UK benefits eligibility rules.

## Researcher

Viviane Verissimo dos Santos, BSc (Hons) Computing, Southampton Solent University.

Supervisor: Dr Edita Gashi.

## Invitation

You are invited to take part in a usability evaluation of VERISSIMO, a web prototype created as part of a final-year computing dissertation. Before deciding, please read this information sheet and ask any questions.

## What Is VERISSIMO?

VERISSIMO is a prototype that helps users understand how selected UK benefits eligibility rules may apply to a fictional scenario. The current scope is Attendance Allowance, Universal Credit and Personal Independence Payment.

The prototype is not an official government service, welfare advice service, legal advice service or financial advice service. It does not make benefit decisions.

## Purpose Of The Study

The study evaluates whether users can:

- complete a guided eligibility questionnaire,
- understand the result categories,
- see why each result was produced,
- find the official GOV.UK source links,
- understand recommended evidence,
- use the simpler explanation and printable report features.

The study is about usability and explainability, not about assessing a participant's real benefit entitlement.

## Who Can Take Part?

Participants should be adults aged 18 or over who can use a web browser and can give informed consent.

Participants should not take part if they are currently seeking urgent benefits advice, expect the researcher to advise them about their real entitlement, or would feel distressed by seeing fictional benefits-related scenarios.

## What Will Happen?

If you agree to take part, you will be asked to:

1. read the study information and confirm consent,
2. use VERISSIMO with fictional scenario data,
3. complete several short tasks,
4. think aloud while using the prototype if comfortable,
5. answer a short post-test questionnaire.

The session is expected to take approximately 30 to 45 minutes.

## What Data Will Be Collected?

The researcher may record:

- whether tasks were completed,
- visible usability issues,
- general comments about clarity and ease of use,
- questionnaire responses,
- anonymised quotes if useful for the dissertation.

The study should not collect real health, income, immigration, benefit claim, address, National Insurance, account or identity details. If such information is accidentally mentioned, it should not be written down in the research notes.

## Risks And Discomfort

The study is low risk, but benefits-related wording may feel sensitive. The researcher will use fictional scenarios, remind participants that the prototype is not advice, and allow participants to skip or stop at any time.

## Benefits

There may be no direct personal benefit. Your feedback may help improve the clarity, accessibility and responsible design of the prototype.
